When kids are ready for more, these free tools are what real programmers use.

Why Learn Python?

Python is one of the easiest real programming languages for kids. The words look almost like English, you can make games, drawings, and simple apps, and it’s used by people at Google, NASA, and game studios. Great next step after Scratch.

  • CodeIt — Python for Kids — Free lessons and games that run in the browser (ages 8–14).
  • MyPy Coding — Free one-on-one Python lessons for grades 2–8.
  • Kids Python — Interactive Python course for grades 3–9 (free preview chapters).
  • Raspberry Pi Code Club Projects — Free step-by-step Python projects for kids.
  • YouTube: Python for kids — Short beginner videos.

Math Cheat Sheets

Quick formulas and free printable sheets for elementary math (especially helpful for 2nd and 5th grade).

Common Formulas

  • Area of a rectangle: length × width
  • Perimeter of a rectangle: 2 × (length + width)
  • Area of a triangle: ½ × base × height
  • Volume of a box: length × width × height
  • Average (mean): add all numbers, then divide by how many numbers there are

What Is AI? What Is Quantum?

Curious about the big ideas? Here are simple explanations and free places to learn more.

What is AI (Artificial Intelligence)?

AI is a computer program that learns from lots of examples, just like you learn from seeing many pictures of cats. It can recognize things, answer questions, or make pictures — but it is not alive and it can make mistakes. Always check what it says!

  • AI Explained Simply for Kids — Easy 25-minute lesson.
  • Google Teachable Machine — Train your own AI with pictures (fun and free).
  • BBC Teach: Introducing AI — Short video for ages 7–11.
  • Machine Learning for Kids — Hands-on projects (great for 5th grade).

What is Quantum Computing?

Normal computers use bits that are either 0 or 1 (like a light switch that is on or off). Quantum computers use special bits called qubits that can be both at the same time — like a coin spinning in the air. This lets them try many answers at once for certain hard problems.

  • Quantum Computer Facts for Kids — Simple encyclopedia page.
  • DIY.org: Quantum Computing Facts for Kids — Easy reading with pictures.
  • Quantum Activity Pack for Kids (PDF) — Free printable activities.
  • YouTube: Quantum for kids — Short fun videos.

Kids Online Safety

This site is made for kids and parents. Here are simple safety tips:

  • Stay with an adult. Kids should use the internet with a parent or trusted adult nearby.
  • Never share personal information. Do not type your real name, school, address, phone number, or photos online.
  • Use the free versions only. All links go to free educational sites. No accounts or payments are needed to start.
  • Check what you see. If something feels wrong or confusing, stop and ask a parent.
  • Be kind online. Treat others the way you want to be treated.

Helpful free resources for parents:

  • Common Sense Media — Reviews and safety tips for kids’ apps and websites.
  • FTC: Protecting Your Child’s Privacy Online — Official safety guidance.

How to Start Today

Simple tips for parents of a 2nd or 5th grader.

  • Pick one thing. Choose either Scratch (coding) or Khan Academy (math) and spend 15–20 minutes together.
  • Sit with them the first time. Kids do better when a parent is nearby for the first session.
  • Celebrate small wins. Finished a Scratch project or a math lesson? High-five and stop. Short sessions work best.
  • Keep it free. All the links above have free versions. No accounts required to start.
